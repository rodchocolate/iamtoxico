cercle brugge ksv

Cercle Brugge Koninklijke Sportvereniging is a professional football club based in Bruges, Belgium, competing in the Belgian Pro League. Founded on 9 April 1899 as Cercle Sportif Brugeois by former students of the Saint Francis Xavier Institute, the club has won three national titles (1911, 1927, and 1930) and the Belgian Cup twice (1927 and 1985). They play home games at the Jan Breydel Stadium, which they share with rivals Club Brugge. The club's history is marked by periods of success and early 20th century championships, followed by a decline and relegation to the Belgian Second Division in 1936, and theon the 1941 season, they finished last but one in the league, but the Belgian football association (KBVB) ruled that no team should be relegated due to the war.

le cercle

Le Cercle is an invitation-only foreign policy forum and think-tank specializing in international security. Established in 1952-1953 as Cercle Pinay by French prime minister Antoine Pinay and intelligence agent Jean Violet, the group's initial focus was the integration of a Christian-Catholic Europe and reconciliation between France and Germany. Over time, the shift in objectives became an emphasis on forming a strong anti-Communist alliance between the United States and Europe. The group is described as an elite, secret conference comparable to the Bilderberg meeting, but with no public or media presence. Its members include high-ranking politicians, diplomats, and intelligence agents, including figures such as Henry Kissinger, Richard Nixon, and Margaret Thatcher.

administrative divisions

The term 'Cercle' is also used as an administrative unit in the French Overseas Empire and the French colonial system, as well as a Malian administrative unit. In Belgium, 'Cercles' refer to student societies based around each faculty.
